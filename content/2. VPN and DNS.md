# Overview
This section explains the importance and functionality of VPNs and private DNS servers, and how to choose and configure them
# Threat Model
Your internet traffic is not private. If you signed up for home internet service in your real name, all traffic on your network is logged by your internet service provider.

When you connect to the internet, your device is assigned an IP address. This is a globally unique identifier that is seen and often recorded by every website you visit on that device. In the US, this identifier is not typically cycled, meaning you could have the same IP attached to your device for years. Unencrypted network traffic can be intercepted in flight or inspected by the service you are connecting to. This poses the following risks -
- IP address can be used to approximate your location to varying degrees of accuracy
- Your ISP can be subpoenaed by authorities to get your personal information and internet history
- Your internet history can be used to build a profile on you
- Information about your home network can be obtained

# Virtual Private Networks (VPN)
A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN server. The VPN server then routes your request to the requested service on your behalf. The website you request data from sees the VPN server's IP address instead of your device's IP address. When using a VPN, your internet traffic will be harder to associate with your IP address, and the details are hidden from your ISP and the services you connect with. You can also bypass censorship and geo-locked content.

|          | You             | Connection                                                                                                   | Website                                             |
| -------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| No VPN   | Real IP Address | Unencrypted,                                                                                                 | ISP and website sees everything                     |
| With VPN | Real IP address | Encrypted connection to VPN server, alias IP address assigned, VPN server connects to website on your behalf | ISP and website sees alias IP address and gibberish |

## When to use a VPN
* Always by default
## When to not use a VPN
* VPNs can cause issues when logging into some banking apps and websites
* VPNs may throw errors during identity verification tasks, like signing up to sensititive services and verifying your identity with ID
* VPNs can cause issues viewing Youtube videos
## When it is critical to use a VPN
* Torrenting or P2P file sharing
* Accessing data-hungry services that would seek to monetize your interaction data or silently cross-identify you
* When connected to a public WiFi network
* Doing anything online you would not want seen by anyone including your government
## Choosing a VPN
VPNs are not fully anonymous, and require a level of trust between you and the provider. The VPN provider knows your real IP address, and their physical location has legal implications that can affect the security and privacy of the connection. You should look for VPNs with the following characteristics.

* No logs - does not store traffic logs
* Encrypted - encrypts in-flight traffic and the VPN servers themselves
* Based in a country outside of the [14 Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKUSA_Agreement) foreign intelligence-sharing agreement
* Will allow you to sign up with an anonymous ProtonMail email address and pay with masked cards or cash

## Choosing Servers
You can connect to any one of hundreds of ProtonVPN servers in the world, and your web traffic will appear to be from that location. Your choice of server country will have different privacy and security implications, and will affect the speed of the connection, but you still get great benefits from using a VPN regardless of the country.

* Clicking "Quick Connect" will connect you to the fastest server, which is likely near you geographically. This option is best for users who prioritize speed and reliability.

For better privacy or higher risk threat models, consider the following:

* Avoid servers in [14 Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKUSA_Agreement) countries.
	* Proton claims to encrypt traffic and store no logs. On the off chance that they don't keep this promise, we can mitigate any potential audit of our internet traffic by routing through servers in countries where surveillance cooperation and data-sharing treaties don't apply.
	* Even though ProtonVPN is headquartered in Switzerland (outside 14 Eyes), servers physically located in 14 Eyes countries may have their traffic monitored at the infrastructure level, or have their servers seized by local intelligence agencies.
* Use [Secure Core](https://protonvpn.com/support/secure-core-vpn) servers - route your VPN traffic through two servers instead of one. Your connection first passes through a high-security server in a privacy-friendly country (Switzerland, Iceland, or Sweden) before exiting through your chosen destination server. If an attacker compromises or monitors the exit VPN server (common in countries with broad surveillance powers like the US or UK), they can only trace traffic back to the Secure Core server, and not to your real IP address.

# DNS
Domain Name Resolution (DNS) servers exist all over the world to translate IP addresses into human-friendly URLs - (64.233.160.0 -> google.com). Queries to these servers are _separate_ from your regular traffic. Typically, your ISP will automatically route DNS requests to their own DNS servers, meaning they log every website you visit, know your identity, and can associate your traffic with you.

Using a VPN mitigates this by routing DNS queries through their own secure servers. With ProtonVPN, this routing happens automatically and is fully encrypted with no logging. This is an adequate solution.

There is another option that has some desirable features. A custom DNS server with NextDNS.

* Blocks Ads, Malware, and Trackers at the domain level, before the connection is even made. This is a deeper level of protection than browser-based blockers
* Custom rules (e.g. requests to all google domains)
* Parental controls (block time wasting sites)
* Functions even when disconnected from VPN
* Very easy to set up
* Virtually free - up to 300,000 requests per month
## NextDNS
### Server Setup
* Create a [NextDNS](https://my.nextdns.io/) account with alias credentials according to [[9. Accounts and Passwords#Creating new accounts]].
* You can choose to create different profiles for different devices. I have one for computers and one for mobile devices. If you want this, create a new profile now.
### Connect your device to the server
* On the home screen of the NextDNS profile, refer to the setup guide for your device to connect.
* Connect to <https://test.nextdns.io/> and verify status - ok
### Connect your web browser to the server
Firefox uses Cloudflare DNS by default, you must tell it to use your own DNS server.

* Go to Firefox settings -> Security and Privacy -> DNS over HTTPS
* Select Max Protection
* In the Choose Provider dropdown, select Custom
* Add your DNS-over-https address from your NextDNS profile home page (e.g. <https://dns.nextdns.io/e8309f>)
### Connect your VPN to the server
VPNs will likely default to their own DNS servers, you must tell them to use yours.

* In ProtonVPN settings, toggle on Custom DNS Servers
* In the Add a New Server field, paste the IPv6 address from your NextDNS profile home page
### Server settings
* In the NextDNS Profile Security tab
	* Enable DNS Rebinding Protection
	* Enable Block Newly Registered Domains (NRDs)
	* Enable Block Dynamic DNS Hostnames
	* Enable Block Parked Domains
* In the Privacy tab
	* Click Add a Blocklist and browse the options, choose any that seem useful. I recommend NextDNS Ads & Trackers Blocklist, OISD, and AdGuard Mobile Ads filter. This is a core feature that blocks ads, trackers, and malware at the domain level.
* In Parental Controls tab
	* Block addictive or time wasting websites
* In Settings tab
	* Disable Logs
	* Clear logs
	* If you want to make another profile with similar settings, you can duplicate it here.

### Logs
A great feature of DNS servers is the ability to view ALL traffic coming from your device. Turn on logs (remember to turn them off when you're done), and play around on a device, you can see every request made by every app on your device. Use this information to create detailed deny lists if you wish.
