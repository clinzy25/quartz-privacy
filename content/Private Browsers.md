# Overview
This is a guide for choosing and customizing a secure browser that is resilient to modern tracking techniques and does not store or send your internet activity anywhere. Usage habits and strategies are discussed to reinforce these protections.
# Threat Model
Your choice of web browser has broad implications regarding your digital privacy and security. Modern web browsers may send all of your internet activity to remote servers indexed by your personal information, regardless of VPN usage, internet provider, or any other privacy techniques in this guide. This data is then sold to numerous third parties. Different web browsers offer different levels of protection against trackers, ads, and fingerprinting, with different settings for controlling your privacy and security.

Web browsers
- Log and store your internet history, credentials, and interaction data to remote severs
- May associate all of this information to your true name if you provide it
- Are containers for cookies and trackers installed by web sites you visit that monitor things like your internet activity, location, and behavior and send it to third parties
- Are able to capture any sensitive information that appears on the screen andsend it to third parties
- May abuse device permissions like location, microphone and camera
- Usually default to settings, tools, and search engines that do not respect privacy

# Threat Response
We want to use an open source browser that is not provided by Google or any company with a weak privacy track record. We will harden the browser by modifying the settings and our own behavior when using it, with the goal of complete privacy and anonymity.
